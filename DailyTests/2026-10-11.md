### Full reference derivation — supplied after hints

Define $`d=x_0-\bar x`$ and $`\mu(x_0)=\alpha+\beta x_0`$. OLS with intercept gives:

```math
\hat\mu(x_0)=\hat\alpha+\hat\beta x_0=\bar Y+d\hat\beta.
```

Since $`\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)=0`$, the slope formula gives:

```math
\hat\beta=\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)Y_i}{S_{xx}}
=\beta+\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)\varepsilon_i}{S_{xx}}.
```

The intercept term cancels and $`\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)x_i=S_{xx}`$. Thus:

```math
Var(\hat\beta)=\frac{\sigma^2}{S_{xx}},\qquad
\bar Y=\alpha+\beta\bar x+\bar\varepsilon,\qquad
Var(\bar Y)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
```

Because errors are independent, cross-observation covariance terms vanish:

```math
Cov(\bar Y,\hat\beta)
=\frac{1}{nS_{xx}}\sum_{i,j}(x_j-\bar x)Cov(\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_j)
=\frac{\sigma^2}{nS_{xx}}\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)=0.
```

Let $`h_0=1/n+d^2/S_{xx}`$. The estimate is unbiased and Gaussian with variance $`\sigma^2h_0`$. Under normal errors, $`RSS/\sigma^2`$ has a chi-square distribution with n-2 degrees of freedom and is independent of the OLS coefficient estimates. Therefore:

```math
\frac{\hat\mu(x_0)-\mu(x_0)}{s\sqrt{h_0}}\sim t_{n-2}.
```

The 95% **mean confidence interval** is:

```math
\hat\mu(x_0)\ \pm\ t_{n-2,0.975}\,s\sqrt{\frac1n+\frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}}.
```

For an independent new observation, $`Y_{new}=\mu(x_0)+\varepsilon_{new}`$. Prediction error equals $`\mu(x_0)-\hat\mu(x_0)+\varepsilon_{new}`$. The new error is independent of training estimates, adding variance $`\sigma^2`$. The 95% **prediction interval** is:

```math
\hat\mu(x_0)\ \pm\ t_{n-2,0.975}\,s\sqrt{1+\frac1n+\frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}}.
```

Both intervals widen away from xbar; prediction intervals are wider because of new-observation noise. Distinguish three quantities:

- $`Var(Y_{new}\mid x_0)=\sigma^2`$: intrinsic noise, constant under homoskedasticity.
- $`Var(\hat\mu(x_0))=\sigma^2h_0`$: variation across repeated training samples.
- $`Var(Y_{new}-\hat\mu(x_0))=\sigma^2(1+h_0)`$: repeated-sampling prediction error, including both sources.

After conditioning on the realized fitted model, its numerical prediction is fixed; the usual frequentist interval statement concerns repeated training/new-observation sampling. Do not describe intrinsic response variance as increasing with distance under these assumptions.

### Modeling implications

Intervals assume the linear conditional mean and constant noise variance remain correct. They quantify parameter estimation and observation noise, not arbitrary misspecification or regime change. More data in the original range reduces estimation uncertainty but does not establish correctness in a new range. Validate prediction-interval coverage by deployment range and regime, with appropriate future data; distinguish mean inference from single-outcome decisions.

The concern extends to ML: standard mean-leaf forests and constant-leaf boosted trees do not learn unrestricted trend extrapolation; neural-model extrapolation depends on structure and training. Low tree disagreement is not automatically a calibrated prediction interval. No universal OLS-style distance formula applies to all ML models. ### Reference answers and learning follow-up

Under the intended full-column-rank setting, kappa2(A)=10/0.01=1000 and kappa2(A^T A)=10^6. Normal equations square conditioning; stable QR or direct SVD avoids explicitly forming the Gram matrix. Ill-conditioning reflects problem sensitivity, not a coding error; stable algorithms do not remove inherent sensitivity. If A were wide with a nontrivial input nullspace, A^T A would instead be singular; the finite identity assumes full column rank.
