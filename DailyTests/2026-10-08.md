## Q1 — Dependency Ordering

### Question

There are n jobs numbered 0 through n-1. Each dependency [a,b] means a must finish before b starts. Write Python 3 returning any valid execution order, or an empty list if impossible. Explain cycle detection and time / space complexity.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **M/P**. Tried to represent dependencies as trees, merge them, and check whether a child became a parent; proposed roughly quadratic time.
- Identified the possibility of contradictory dependencies but did not retrieve directed graphs / topological sort or produce a working implementation. Mentioning inability to implement within 9 minutes does not establish actual elapsed time.

### Retrieval Cue

Use edges a → b. Kahn's algorithm stores adjacency lists and indegrees, queues all zero-indegree jobs, then removes their outgoing edges. Each removed job satisfies all prerequisites. If fewer than n jobs are processed, the remaining graph contains a cycle. Time and space: $O(V+E)$. This is feedback, not an independently completed solution.

## Q2 — HH versus HT Waiting Times

### Question

Flip a fair coin until HH first appears. Find the expected number of flips. Repeat for HT and explain why the expectations differ, without simulation.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **P/Y**. Recalled a value of 4 but could not reconstruct the absorbing-state method; the HH answer and comparison were not established correctly.
- Feedback supplied $E[T_{HH}]=6$ and $E[T_{HT}]=4$, using suffix states and then an absorbing-chain matrix.

### Retrieval Cue

Let $E_0$ denote no useful suffix and $E_H$ a trailing H. Both satisfy $E_0=1+E_0/2+E_H/2$. For HH, $E_H=1+E_0/2$; for HT, $E_H=1+E_H/2$. In the HT problem, another H preserves progress; in HH, T resets it.

With transient states ordered as (empty, H):

$$
Q_{HH}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Q_{HT}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad t=\mathbf1+Qt=(I-Q)^{-1}\mathbf1.
$$

## Q3 — Exponential MLE and Fisher Information

### Question

For n independent positive interarrival times from an exponential distribution with unknown rate lambda, derive the MLE from the density and then derive its asymptotic standard error.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **P/M**. Could not recall the MLE derivation. Follow-up questions exposed unfamiliarity with the score, Fisher information, asymptotic normality, and the inverse-information standard error.
- The following derivation was taught afterward; it was not a cold solve.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
f(x;\lambda)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x},\quad x\gt0,\quad\lambda\gt0,
\qquad \ell(\lambda)=n\log\lambda-\lambda\sum_iX_i.
$$

$$
s_n(\lambda)=\ell'(\lambda)=\frac n\lambda-\sum_iX_i,
\qquad \hat\lambda=\frac n{\sum_iX_i}=\frac1{\bar X}.
$$

The score is the slope of log-likelihood. Under regularity conditions, $I_1(\lambda)=E[s_1^2]=-E[\ell_1'']=1/\lambda^2$ and $I_n=nI_1$. Thus:

$$
\sqrt n(\hat\lambda-\lambda)\xrightarrow{d}N(0,\lambda^2),
\qquad \widehat{SE}(\hat\lambda)=\frac{\hat\lambda}{\sqrt n}.
$$

Inverse information gives asymptotic variance, not an exact finite-sample variance. The curvature identity requires regularity; parameter-dependent support can invalidate the usual argument.

## Q4 — Minimize the Largest Group Sum

### Question

Partition a list of positive integers into at most k nonempty contiguous groups to minimize the largest group sum. For nums = [7,2,5,10,8] and k = 2, return 18. Write efficient Python 3 and explain correctness and complexity.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **P/Y**. Proposed prefix sums, scanning all splits when k = 2, and DP for larger k.
- Did not retrieve binary search on the answer with greedy feasibility. The supplied solution was feedback, not an independent implementation.

### Retrieval Cue

Binary-search a limit M between max(nums) and sum(nums). Greedily extend each group until the next element would exceed M, then open a group. For positive integers, taking the longest feasible prefix minimizes the number of groups. Feasible means at most k groups; increasing M preserves feasibility. Search for the first feasible integer limit. Time $O(n\log(S+1))$, where S is the sum; auxiliary space $O(1)$. Assume a nonempty list and $k\ge1$.

## Q5 — LASSO versus Ridge

### Question

Predict daily Treasury yield changes using 50 strongly correlated features and 300 observations. LASSO selects one member of a correlated cluster, but its selection changes across training windows; Ridge coefficients are more stable. Explain why, choose a method for prediction, say when the alternative is preferable, and identify one deployment failure mode.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **G/Y**. Correctly associated LASSO with dropping variables, Ridge with smoother retained weights, and preferred Ridge. Core intuition mostly correct.
- Feedback sharpened the distinction: L1 sparsity can make selection among similar predictors unstable; L2 shrinkage tends to distribute weight and stabilize weakly identified directions. Prefer LASSO when sparsity / interpretability matters; compare predictive performance using rolling out-of-sample validation.
- Regime / covariance changes can undermine historical predictive performance. That deployment example was supplied in feedback.

### Retrieval Cue

Correlated substitutes can exchange LASSO selection across samples. Ridge trades bias for lower variance; it does not simply retain only features explaining the most y variance. Standardize features and tune regularization within the training procedure.

## Q6 — Bayesian Coin Posterior and Prediction

### Question

Select coin A or B with equal probability. A has head probability 1/2; B has head probability 3/4. After observing HHH from the selected coin, derive the posterior probability of B and the probability that the next flip is H.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **G**. Correctly gave 27/35 and the posterior-weighted next-flip expression; no substantive correction needed.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
P(B\mid HHH)=\frac{(3/4)^3}{(3/4)^3+(1/2)^3}=\frac{27}{35},
\qquad P(H_4\mid HHH)=\frac{27}{35}\frac34+\frac8{35}\frac12=\frac{97}{140}.
$$

Update the hidden coin probability first, then average conditional head probabilities using the posterior.

## Q7 — Projection Matrices and PSD

### Question

A real symmetric matrix P satisfies $P^2=P$. What are its possible eigenvalues? Must $x^\top Px\ge0$ hold for every x? Justify both conclusions.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **Y**. Correct eigenvalues 0 and 1. Initially answered no to PSD, proposing the zero matrix as a counterexample.
- Zero gives equality and is positive semidefinite. The missing distinction was nonnegative versus strictly positive, together with the projection identity.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
\lambda^2=\lambda\Rightarrow\lambda\in\{0,1\},
\qquad x^\top Px=x^\top P^\top Px=\|Px\|^2\ge0.
$$

The identity uses both symmetry and idempotence. Such P is an orthogonal projection; idempotence alone does not justify this quadratic-form identity.

## Q8 — Paired Strategy-Return Test

### Question

Strategies A and B have returns on the same 50 trading days. Test whether expected returns differ: paired or independent two-sample test? Write the statistic and explain how positive correlation between same-day returns affects its standard error.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **G/Y**. Correctly chose a paired test on $D_t=A_t-B_t$ and proposed a t-statistic. Initially unsure whether positive correlation made the test ineffective.
- Correction: holding marginal variances fixed, positive same-day covariance lowers the variance of differences and the standard error under independent-day assumptions.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
H_0:E[D_t]=0,\qquad t=\frac{\bar D}{s_D/\sqrt{50}},
\qquad Var(D_t)=Var(A_t)+Var(B_t)-2Cov(A_t,B_t).
$$

For iid normal differences, compare to a t distribution with 49 degrees of freedom. Same-day cross-strategy correlation is distinct from serial dependence across dates. If differences are serially dependent, use an appropriate HAC standard error or block-based inference rather than assuming iid days.
