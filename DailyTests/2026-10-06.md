## Q7 — Linear Algebra and Optimization Lightning Round

### Questions

1. Why is $A=X^\top X+\lambda I$, with $\lambda\gt0$, invertible even if $X^\top X$ is singular?
2. What is the unconstrained optimum of the following objective?

$$
\max_w \mu^\top w-\frac{\gamma}{2}w^\top\Sigma w
$$

3. If $\mathbf{1}^\top w=1$ is added, what is the first thing to introduce?

### Answer and Feedback

- Answered using nonzero eigenvalues, but did not state the full positive-semidefinite / eigenvalue-shift argument cleanly.
- Gave $(\gamma\Sigma)^{-1}\mu$, which was accepted.
- Answered “KKT?”; feedback preferred “introduce a Lagrange multiplier and set up the Lagrangian” for this equality constraint.
- Self-assessment: **Y/G** — the direction is known, but clear formulas are not yet automatic.

### Retrieval Cues

Since $X^\top X$ is positive semidefinite, every eigenvalue is nonnegative. Adding $\lambda I$ shifts every eigenvalue strictly above zero:

$$
\lambda_i(X^\top X+\lambda I)=\lambda_i(X^\top X)+\lambda\gt0.
$$

For positive risk aversion $\gamma\gt0$ and an invertible covariance matrix $\Sigma$, the optimum is

$$
w^*=(\gamma\Sigma)^{-1}\mu=\frac{1}{\gamma}\Sigma^{-1}\mu.
$$

For the equality constraint, introduce a Lagrange multiplier.
