## Q1 — Shortest Subarray with Sum at Least K

### Question

Given an integer array nums, which may contain negative values, and an integer $K\gt0$, return the length of the shortest nonempty contiguous subarray whose sum is at least K, or -1 if none exists. Example: [2, -1, 2, 1], K = 3 returns 2. Target $O(n)$ time. Explain the invariant, write Python 3, and explain why an ordinary two-pointer sliding window may fail.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **P/Y**. Reached prefix sums but got stuck on pointer movement; subsequently practiced and explicitly reported unfamiliarity with deque.
- Attempt used r = len(nums) as an array index, subtracted nums instead of prefix sums, and used l-r instead of r-l.
- Negatives destroy ordinary sliding-window monotonicity. Main gap: retrieving and implementing the monotonic-deque invariant.
- Follow-up explanations covered the leading zero prefix, why a newer smaller prefix dominates an older larger one, and why checking candidates before appending the current index excludes empty subarrays even if K is nonpositive.

### Retrieval Cue

Define $P_0=0$ and use $P_j-P_i\ge K$, minimizing $j-i$. Maintain increasing prefix values at increasing deque indices. Pop valid starts from the front while updating the answer; pop dominated starts from the back when the current prefix is no larger; append the current index last. Each index enters and leaves at most once.

## Q3 — Measurement Error, Robust SE, and IV

### Question

The true model is $y=\alpha+\beta x+\epsilon$, but only $z=x+u$ is observed. Assume x, u, and epsilon are mutually independent, $E[u]=E[\epsilon]=0$, $\operatorname{Var}(x)=4$, $\operatorname{Var}(u)=1$, and $\beta=0.5$. Regress y on z with an intercept.

1. Derive the probability limit of the OLS slope from covariance divided by variance.
2. Is the bias toward or away from zero?
3. Would heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors fix it?
4. Under what conditions could an instrumental variable help?

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **P+/Y-G**. Cold-derived the reliability ratio $4/5$ multiplying beta; correctly identified attenuation toward zero.
- Correctly recognized that robust SE address nonconstant variance, not coefficient inconsistency.
- IV answer described a possible use case rather than precise validity conditions. This was the main articulation gap.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
\operatorname{plim}\hat\beta
=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(z,y)}{\operatorname{Var}(z)}
=\frac{\beta\operatorname{Var}(x)}
{\operatorname{Var}(x)+\operatorname{Var}(u)}
=\frac45\beta=0.4.
$$

For an instrument W, state relevance and validity: it predicts the true x, is uncorrelated with both measurement error u and structural error epsilon, and affects y only through x. These conditions imply correlation with z and orthogonality to the observed-regression error $\epsilon-\beta u$.

## Q4 — Longest Balanced Binary Subarray

### Question

Given a binary array, return the maximum length of a contiguous subarray containing equal numbers of zeros and ones. Example: [0, 1, 0, 0, 1, 1, 0] returns 6. Target $O(n)$ time and $O(n)$ space. Explain the invariant, write Python 3, and test one edge case.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **P/Y**. Identified prefix counts / a running balance and correctly noticed that two pointers lacked a reliable movement rule.
- Did not independently retrieve the transformation 0 → -1, 1 → +1, followed by a hash of each balance's first occurrence.
- No completed independent implementation or edge-case test was recorded; the interviewer supplied the solution.

### Retrieval Cue

Equal counts become a zero-sum subarray. Repeated prefix balances identify valid intervals. Store the earliest occurrence only to maximize length, initialized with first = {0: -1}; at index i, use i - first[balance]. Do not overwrite an earlier index.

## Q5 — Discrete Median and Symmetry

### Question

Choose three distinct integers uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,10\}$ and sort them as $X_{(1)}\lt X_{(2)}\lt X_{(3)}$. Find $P(X_{(2)}=6)$ and $E[X_{(2)}]$ without brute-force enumeration.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **G**; actual time: **1 minute**, explicitly reported by the user.
- Gave $5\cdot4/\binom{10}{3}=1/6$ and guessed the correct expectation 5.5.
- Interviewer described this as clean cold retrieval. The user subsequently requested a derivation of the expectation; symmetry and the general order-statistic formula were supplied afterward. Preserve the Green rating without claiming the full justification was independently given.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
P(X_{(2)}=6)=\frac{\binom51\binom41}{\binom{10}{3}}=\frac16.
$$

The bijection $x\mapsto11-x$ maps median m to 11-m and preserves sample probabilities, so $E[X_{(2)}]=11/2$. More generally, a uniform k-element subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has $E[X_{(r)}]=r(N+1)/(k+1)$.

## Q6 — Five-Day Labels and Walk-Forward Validation

### Question

A rates signal predicts the next five trading days' cumulative return. Train through T and test from T+1; validation looks excellent. Identify two contamination risks despite chronological ordering, give a concrete safeguard for each, and name a trading-oriented metric alongside predictive fit.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **G-ish**. Identified training targets extending into the test period and contamination associated with previous days' forward-return labels.
- Suggested delaying test start to around T+5 and chose maximum drawdown.
- Feedback accepted the overlap / purge-and-embargo direction and distinguished train/test boundary leakage from dependence among adjacent test labels. The full block / overlap-aware inference safeguard was supplied in feedback.

### Retrieval Cue

1. **Boundary leakage:** a label dated t uses returns through t+5. Purge training rows whose label windows overlap the test window and ensure labels are available at training time. Set any gap from exact information intervals; “start at T+5” alone is not a universal boundary rule.
2. **Dependent test observations:** adjacent five-day labels share four return days. Use non-overlapping test blocks or overlap-aware uncertainty estimates such as block bootstrap / appropriate HAC. Dependence alone is not proof of leakage, but treating it as iid can overstate precision.

Maximum drawdown was the user's metric. Sharpe, turnover, and PnL after costs were additional interviewer suggestions.

## Q7 — Global Minimum-Variance Portfolio

### Question

For symmetric positive-definite Sigma, solve

$$
\min_w w^\top\Sigma w
\quad\text{subject to}\quad \mathbf1^\top w=1.
$$

Give the closed-form optimizer and one sentence explaining uniqueness.

### Answer and Feedback

- Result: **Y/G**. Asked to see the first-order condition to check the derivative, then confirmed that their own first step was correct and said they could derive lambda.
- Needed prompting to express w through lambda, impose the budget constraint, and finish the closed form.
- Record a retrieval / completion gap, not a demonstrated inability to differentiate the quadratic. The interviewer supplied the final formula and uniqueness explanation.

### Retrieval Cue

$$
L=w^\top\Sigma w+\lambda(\mathbf1^\top w-1),
\qquad 2\Sigma w+\lambda\mathbf1=0.
$$

$$
w=-\frac{\lambda}{2}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1,
\qquad
\lambda=-\frac{2}{\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1},
\qquad
w^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}{\mathbf1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1}.
$$

Positive-definite Sigma makes the quadratic strictly convex; the affine budget constraint therefore admits a unique minimizer.
